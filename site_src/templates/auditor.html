Bill & Act Drafting Compliance Auditor

Upload a drafted bill or statutory clauses and benchmark them against national and supranational legislative drafting manuals. Produces a 1–10 compliance score, dimensional sub-scores, a rule-by-rule checklist with official manual citations, and concrete redraft suggestions.

How the auditor works

The auditor is a rule engine that runs entirely in your browser (assets/auditor.js). Your text is not uploaded anywhere unless you add an AI provider key below. The same engine is available offline as scripts/evaluate_bill.py.

Steps

  1. Read the text. XML or HTML tags (Akoma Ntoso, USLM) are stripped, the text is split into sentences, and any recitals (lines from Whereas or Having regard to up to the enacting formula or the first section) are separated from the operative provisions.
  2. Load a rule profile. The ten featured guidelines have hand-written profiles: legal tradition, policy on shall, target average sentence length (26–32 words), recitals policy, and the manual sections cited in results. Every other catalogued guideline gets a profile derived from its country code and jurisdiction level (common law, civil law, bijural, supranational or international).
  3. Run the checks. Each check produces PASS, WARN or FAIL and deducts points from one or more sub-scores, each starting at 10.
  4. Score. Sub-scores are clamped to 1–10 and combined into the overall score.

Scoring

Overall score = Clarity × 25% + Normative verbs × 25% + Definitions × 20% + Conventions × 15% + Structure × 15%. The exact index is shown to one decimal place and rounded for the headline score. Bands: 8.0 and above is high compliance, 5.5–7.9 moderate, below 5.5 low. Each flagged provision comes with a suggested redraft generated from the same rule (for example, shall rewritten as must).

Comparison and AI critique

Ticking guidelines under Multi-Jurisdiction Comparison runs the same text through each profile, so the same draft can score differently under the UK OPC (which discourages shall) and the EU Joint Practical Guide (which expects it in articles). The optional AI critique sends the first 12,000 characters of the text and the guideline name to Gemini (gemini-2.5-flash) or OpenAI (gpt-4o-mini) with your key and shows the response separately; it does not change the score.

Limitations

The checks are pattern matches on English text. They do not assess legal substance, policy or constitutional validity, and they can miss problems or flag acceptable usage. Drafts in other languages are scored, but most checks look for English wording. Treat the score as a drafting-style indicator for review by counsel.
