Article 1. Structure of Regulations

  • 1.1. VAC organization; numbering system.

A. The Virginia Administrative Code is organized into 24 broad subject matter titles. An agency’s regulations are organized into a title, according to the responsibility and nature of the agency. Within each title, the agencies are alphabetically ordered and numbered by multiples of five. This system allows for agencies to be added or reordered and still be organized providing plenty of space for new agencies within a title.

B. The chapter numbering in VAC is organized by multiples of 10. This design is to accommodate new chapters within a title. Chapters are cited by using the title number, agency number, and chapter number. Do not cite a VAC chapter (i.e., a "regulation") by using a citation to the first section of a chapter followed by "et seq." EXAMPLE INCORRECT: 4VAC5-30-10 et seq. INCORRECT: 4VAC5-30, Virginia State Parks Regulations. CORRECT: Virginia State Parks Regulations (4VAC5-30) C. The section is the basic unit of VAC. The content of a section is as short and simple presentation of a single regulatory function as possible. Sections are numbered in increments of 10, leaving room to add sections and organize the new section in a logical topical manner. Each section of a regulation is identified with its own VAC codification number. The numbering system of VAC is as follows:

i VAC ii-iii-iv

  • 1. i represents the subject matter title (1 through 24) where the regulation is located.
  • 2. ii represents the promulgating agency.
  • 3. iii represents the chapter number within the title.
  • 4. iv represents the section number within the chapter. EXAMPLE 11VAC10-100-40. Names of horses. Title 11 - Gaming Agency 10 - Virginia Racing Commission Chapter 100 - Horses Section 40 - Names of horses D. A section number may not be repurposed to contain a subject different from its initial subject. Therefore, do not strike all language in a section and replace it with content of a different subject. Instead, repeal the current section and add a new section where it topically falls within the subject matter of the chapter.

E. Generally, existing sections may not be renumbered once established in VAC to preserve the integrity of a chapter’s original numbering system. If major reorganization is needed, repeal the current chapter and replace it with a new chapter. The exception to the rule on renumbering is when a regulation is transferred from one agency to another due to a change in regulatory authority.

F. Questions concerning the (i) numbering of a chapter (regulation) within a regulatory catalog or sections within a regulation or (ii) transfer of chapters or sections from one agency to another should be directed to the Registrar’s Office.

  • 1.2. Arrangement of a regulation.

A. Concise drafting of a regulation and logical arrangement of the sections, subsections, and subdivisions in a regulation create a general design best suited to communicate regulatory content. The major objective in arranging text within a regulation is to make the document as clear and understandable as possible.

B. Use the following sequence of elements as a guideline when preparing a new regulation or amending a current regulation:

  • 1. Place generally applicable provisions before specialized provisions.
  • 2. Place permanent provisions before temporary provisions.
  • 3. Place more important provisions before less important provisions.
  • 4. Place frequently used provisions before less frequently or rarely applicable provisions. C. A regulation may be divided into "parts" for the purpose of grouping like portions of the regulation. If necessary, a part may be divided into "articles."
  • 1. When dividing a chapter into parts, first add all sections of the chapter in the Regulation Information System (RIS), then go back an insert the parts so that a first section and a last section can be identified in the system. Parts are numbered using Roman numerals and titled using title case punctuation, that is, each significant word is initial capped (given a a capital letter). For example, "Part IV Designation of Specialty Certification." Do not create a part to contain only one section. Do not create only one part for a regulation.
  • 2. When dividing a part into articles, use RIS to identify first and last sections contained in an article. Articles are numbered using Arabic numerals and titled using title case punctuation. For example, "Article 2 Apprentice Level Certification." Do not create an article to contain only one section. Do not create only one article in a part.
  • 1.3. Sections, subsections, and subdivisions.

A. A section is the basic unit of VAC. The content of a section is a short, simple presentation of a single regulatory function. Sections within a chapter are numbered incrementally by 10.

B. Divide separate requirements concerning a single subject, that is, a section, into subsections. If a section contains more than one subsection, label each subsection using an uppercase letter.

C. A list of requirements or standards is clearer and more readable if put into the form of numbered subdivisions. Before dividing a subsection into a list of subdivisions, introduce the list with a complete thought followed by a colon. The initial level of subdivisions is indented and numbered 1, 2, 3, etc., and each subdivision at that outline level is numbered and placed on a separate line. Subdivisions may be further divided into subdivisions. The next (second) subsequent division of a subdivision is indented again and labeled with a, b, c, etc. Use the following organizational system in the text of regulations:

  • 1. Designate subsections by uppercase letters of the alphabet (i.e., A, B, C, etc.).
  • 2. Designate subdivisions in numeric and alphabetic order. The order of precedence follows and is illustrated in Figure 1.3 C: A, B, C; 1, 2, 3; a, b, c; (1), (2), (3); (a), (b), (c). If a subsection or subdivision catchline must be used, it should be in sentence case and followed by a period, and a subsection or subdivision catchline must be used for every subsection or subdivision of the outline level carrying a catchline.
  • 1.4. Section catchlines.

A. The name given to each section is called a catchline. The catchline should briefly and accurately summarize or describe the subject matter of the section. If it does not, expand the catchline or divide the contents of the section into multiple sections. Avoid wording a catchline using "General" and "Miscellaneous." Those words are not specific. Do not include "etc." in a catchline.

B. The section catchline follows the section number, forming the first line of a section. The catchline is separate from the section text. Do not use acronyms in a catchline. Capitalize only the first letter and proper names. (Use sentence case, i.e., only initial cap the first letter of the first word.) Separate items in a catchline with semicolons, and end each catchline with a period.

C. A change in a catchline requires amendment in the same format as required for body text (i.e., stricken text for deletions and underscored text for new language).

D. The catchline is not regulatory text. The catchline summarizes the subject of each section, serving like a table of contents for a regulation. Draft regulatory text of the section so that its meaning is clear independent of the catchline. Essential information contained in a catchline must be repeated in the body of the section.

EXAMPLES 18VAC60-21-220. Inactive licensure. 12VAC30-135-410. Administration; authority; waived provisions.

  • 1.5. Severability provision; purpose statement.

A. Section 2.2-4004 of the Code of Virginia provides that regulatory provisions adopted under the Administrative Process Act (APA) (§ 2.2-4000 of the Code of Virginia) are generally severable. This section eliminates the need for agencies to include a severability clause within a regulation. When an agency amends a regulation that currently contains a severability clause, this clause should be repealed. This section does not apply to regulations that are adopted exempt from the APA.

B. Pursuant to 1VAC7-10-40 C, the Registrar of Regulations may omit from publication in the Register and VAC, specific examples of nonregulatory text, including purpose statements.

  • 1.6. Definitions section.

A. Place the definition section at the beginning of the chapter, generally as the first numbered section. Definitions provide clarification for terms used within the chapter and allow the regulation writer to control the meaning of a word. They also save space in the body of the regulation. For example, "department" may be defined to mean the Department of Social Services and "TANF" to be the acronym for the Temporary Assistance to Needy Families program. If appropriate, defined terms may also be placed within and limited to a section or part.

B. Define a term only when the meaning is important and the term is used more than once in the regulation. If a term is only used once, define the term in the text where it's used. Do not define terms not used in the regulation. Pursuant to 1VAC7-10-40 C, the Registrar of Regulations may omit from publication in the Register and VAC, specific examples of nonregulatory text, including defined terms that are not used in the regulatory text of a chapter.

C. Do not include substantive provisions or requirements within the definitions of terms in the definitions section. For example, "executive secretary" may be defined in the definitions section, but do not include the required duties for the position as part of the definition. The duties must be the subject of a separate section.

D. Use the following guidelines for formatting definitions:

  • 1. Use the following language for the introductory paragraph: "The following words and terms when used in this chapter (or part, article, or section) shall have the following meanings unless the context clearly indicates otherwise:"
  • 2. Arrange the defined terms in alphabetical order.
  • 3. Capitalize only the first letter of the first word of the tem that is being defined unless the term is a proper noun. Use standard grammatical capitalization rules in definition text; only proper nouns are initial capped. Wherever possible, use the singular form of the word for a defined term.
  • 4. Place the word or term being defined within quotation marks.
  • 6. Immediately after the word or term being defined, insert the word "means" followed by the text of the definition.
  • 7. Avoid using dashes, slashes, or parentheses in a term being defined. For example, use "Individualized education plan" or "IEP" means... instead of "Individualized education plan (IEP)" means.... EXAMPLE 9VAC25-600-10. Definitions. The following words and terms when used in this chapter shall have the following meanings unless the context clearly indicates otherwise: "Act" means the Ground Water Management Act of 1992 (§ 62.1-254 et seq. of the Code of Virginia). "Board" means the State Water Control Board. "Department" means the Department of Environmental Quality. "Groundwater" means any water, except capillary moisture, beneath the land surface in the zone of saturation or beneath the bed of any stream, lake, reservoir or other body of surface water wholly or partially within the boundaries of this Commonwealth, whatever the subsurface geologic structure in which such water stands, flows, percolates or otherwise occurs. "Groundwater management area" means a geographically defined groundwater area in which the board has deemed the levels, supply or quality of groundwater to be adverse to public welfare, health and safety.
  • 1.7. Stating an effective date.

A. Generally, do not include an effective date in the text of a regulation. Pursuant to 1VAC710-40 C, the Registrar of Regulations may omit from publication in the Register and VAC, specific examples of nonregulatory text, including effective date clauses.

If it is necessary to refer to an effective date in the text and the exact effective date for the regulation is unknown (i.e., the text is in a proposed regulation instead of a final or fast-track), use "(insert effective date of this regulation)" as a placeholder. Be sure to use parentheses, not brackets, for the temporary language. When the final regulation stage is submitted, strike the parenthetical and add the effective date. Brackets are used at the final stage to show the change from the proposed text published in the Register.

EXAMPLE In the proposed: a. Supervisees who began a supervised experience before (insert effective date of this regulation) shall complete the supervised experience by (insert 60 months after the effective date). In the final with an effective date of February 19, 2020: a. Supervisees who began a supervised experience before [ (insert effective date of this regulation) February 19, 2020, ] shall complete the supervised experience by [ (insert 60 months after the effective date) February 19, 2025 ]. B. To state an effective date, use "become" rather than "be." The use of "be" limits the action to one day.

EXAMPLE CORRECT: shall become effective on March 20, 2006 INCORRECT: ...shall be effective on March 20, 2006 C. In the rare case where an individual section in a regulatory action has a different effective date from the other sections in the action, indicate the alternate effective date with language in the text of the section, not in the catchline. For example, the first line in the section might read: "The provisions of this section shall become effective September 1, 2026." Or, the first sentence may begin with "Effective September 1, 2026..." This language may be stricken in a later regulatory action when it is no longer needed to distinguish it from the remaining sections in the chapter.

D. Using "through" in a date range, includes the final day of the range. Using "to" when referring to dates excludes the final day referenced in the date range.

EXAMPLES The permit is effective from March 10, 2005, to March 10, 2026. In this example, the permit is NOT effective on March 10, 2026. The permit is effective from March 10, 2005, through March 9, 2026. This sentence has the same meaning as the first example. Both are correct.

  • 1.8. Special sections; forms and documents incorporated by reference.

A. Do not use appendices as part of a regulation. VAC does not use footnotes, except in the case of footnotes for a table. Material important enough to be set out for enforcement should be made part of the regulation itself and given a section number accordingly. Provisions to be enforced should be included in the regulatory text of the regulation.

B. An unnumbered section titled "FORMS" may appear after the last section of a chapter. The section content is a list of the forms required in administering the chapter. Each form name on the list is a link to an electronic version of the form that is stored within the RIS.

  • 1. Forms are filed with the Registrar pursuant to § 2.2-4031 A of the Code of Virginia and Part IV (1VAC7-10-180 et seq.) of Regulations for Filing and Publishing Agency Regulations (1VAC7-10).
  • 2. Forms must have an effective or revised date on the form, and that date needs to be included with the form name for the list. Forms are uploaded to the FORMS lists using RIS.
  • 3. Pursuant to 1VAC7-10-190 A, an agency may request an exemption from filing a form if the form (i) may be completed only online or electronically or (ii) is not in a printable format. The Request for File a Form by Description, RR12 Form, is available on the Register website. C. An unnumbered section titled "DOCUMENTS INCORPORATED BY REFERENCE (DIBR)" may appear at the end of a chapter. The section content is a list of the documents incorporated by reference (IBR) into the chapter. Generally, IBR is a tool that allows an agency to make requirements or provisions published by another entity an enforceable part of the agency's regulation without reprinting the entire text of the requirement or provision in the regulation. Section 2.2-4103 of the Code of Virginia requires an agency to file the full text of all currently operative regulations with the Registrar's Office; therefore, where regulations adopt text contained in a DIBR, the incorporated material becomes a part of the regulation just as if the material were set out in full in the regulation. For this reason, DIBRs must be filed with the Registrar's Office. The version of the document that is identified in the regulation is the text that is incorporated into the regulation. If an updated version of the incorporated material becomes available, the regulation must be updated through a regulatory action if the agency wants the regulation to incorporate the new version.

Part III (1VAC7-10-140 et seq.) of Regulations for Filing and Publishing Agency Regulations (1VAC7-10) addresses requirements for using IBR and provides limited exceptions to the filing requirements.

  • 1. When filing the incorporated document, the agency must provide a complete copy of the document, either physically or electronically, to the Registrar's Office. If this is a copyrighted document, and the agency does not have permission from the authorial entity to make the document openly available for public reproduction, the document will not be linked on the DIBR list, but the copy retained by the Registrar's Office will be available for the public to read upon demand. The agency must retain a copy, either physically or electronically, for the same public availability.
  • 2. Along with the title of the document on the DIBR list, the following information must be included: (i) version or edition number; (ii) date of document, which may be the effective, adoption, revision, issue, or publication date; (iii) authorial entity; and (iv) contact information for the authorial entity.
  • 3. When a DIBR on the list is sited within regulatory text, the title of the document and associated date provided on the DIBR list must be included in the regulatory text citation.
  • 4. Pursuant to 1VAC7-10-170 C, an agency may request an exemption from filing a DIBR. The Request for File a DIBR by Description, RR11 Form, is available on the Register website. Incorporation of statute from (i) the Virginia Administrative Code, (ii) the Code of Virginia,
  • (iii) the United States Code (USC), (iv) the Code of Federal Regulations (CFR), or (v) the Federal Register (i.e., has not yet been published in the CFR) does not require filing as a DIBR, and those requirements update as the cited requirements update. However, if an agency has adopted federal statute or regulation effective a specific date, that date must be updated through regulatory action.

If a document is mentioned or named in a regulation but the contents of the document are not made requirements of the regulation, the document is not incorporated by reference and would not be filed with the Registrar's Office.

Article 2. Composition and Style Guidelines for the Virginia Register and VAC

  • 2.1. Application of composition and style guidelines.

A. The composition and style guidelines in this manual provide editorial assistance in drafting regulations, general notices, and other items to be published in the Virginia Register of Regulations or the Virginia Administrative Code. The goal is greater clarity and accuracy in the texts of official documents.

B. For specific questions not covered within this style guide, refer to the following reference books concerning matters of style, spelling, usage, and word division:

  • 1. The Chicago Manual of Style online; and
  • 2. Merriam-Webster.com Dictionary.
  • 2.2. Rules of construction.

In the drafting of regulations, the rules of construction and definitions in 1VAC7-10-30 shall apply.

  • 2.3. General construction guidelines.

A. In general, keep the language of the text as clear and simple as possible. When drafting, remember that documents should be written so that the general public can understand them. Avoid using language that only individuals with specialized knowledge can understand. Consistency of expression, logical arrangement, and adherence to accepted usage aid readability.

B. Strive for consistency of terminology, expression, and arrangement. Avoid using the same word or term in more than one sense. Conversely, avoid using different words to denote the same idea. Apply the principles of consistency to phrases, sentences, paragraphs, arrangement, and format. For example, in the text of a regulation, two or more subdivisions that are similar in substance should be parallel in form.

C. In general, use parallel construction for similar levels of provisions. Whether using lists and series or sentences, each list or sentence portion must be of the same grammatical style and content (e.g., part of speech, verb tense, noun number) and relate to a common theme. Following is a simple example:

EXAMPLE CORRECT: The committee reviewed the requirements for determining claims and making payments. INCORRECT: The committee reviewed the requirements for determining claims and how payments must be made.

D. In general, keep a subsection or subdivision simple and short to avoid confusion and misunderstanding. Avoid long sentences where short ones will suffice.

  • 2.3.1. Use active voice sentence construction.

The subject performs the action described by the verb in a sentence using active voice. In a sentence using passive voice, the subject is acted upon by the verb. Use active voice wherever possible.

EXAMPLE CORRECT: The chairman shall appoint members of the committee. INCORRECT: Members of the committee shall be appointed by the chairman.

  • 2.3.2. Use the third person point of view.

Use third person, rather than first person or second person, point of view. Use a noun as the subject so that the regulation is clear as to who must perform the action or meet the requirement.

EXAMPLE CORRECT: The applicant shall file the appropriate forms. INCORRECT: You shall file the appropriate forms.

  • 2.3.3. Use positive construction.

If an idea can be accurately expressed either positively or negatively, express it positively. Negative construction makes reading comprehension more difficult; positive construction communicates the same ideas in a more direct and clear fashion. Negative construction is appropriate where a provision expresses a prohibition. Negative words should not be used where provisions provide only advisory guidance.

EXAMPLE POSITIVE: No licensee shall work outside of the scope of licensure. NEGATIVE: A licensee shall not work outside the scope of licensure. POSITIVE: An application fee will not be prorated. NEGATIVE: No application fee will be prorated.

  • 2.3.4. Use singular number.

Draft regulations using the singular of each noun instead of plural nouns, unless plural construction is necessary to support accurate meaning. Pursuant to subdivision 3 of 1VAC7-1030, "A word used in the singular includes the plural, and a word used in the plural includes the singular unless the context clearly indicates otherwise." If both versions of a word are used in regulatory text, the Registrar's Office will let stand the version best conforming to context and strike or remove the other version. Agencies are encouraged to make this conforming amendment when amending text generally.

EXAMPLE PREFERRED: All information associated with an applicant or a recipient that could disclose an individual's identity... AVOID: All information associated with applicants or recipients that could disclose individuals' identities...

  • 2.3.5. Use gender neutral nouns.

A. Instead of using a pronoun, use the noun that the pronoun would replace, that is, the antecedent of the pronoun. "Licensee," "certificate holder," and "applicant" are examples of commonly replaced nouns in regulatory text. Subdivision 2 of 1VAC7-10-30 prescribes pronoun use for regulatory text.

EXAMPLE b. If the individual is living with a primary caregiver, who is providing the service voluntarily and without pay, and the primary caregiver indicates that he the primary caregiver can no longer care for the individual... B. Except when otherwise required for context, standardize use of the following genderneutral terms. Change:

"husband" or "wife" to "spouse" "father" or "mother" to "parent" "grandfather" or "grandmother" to "grandparent" "stepfather/step-father" or "stepmother/step-mother" to "stepparent" "father-in-law" or "mother-in-law" to "parent-in-law" "son" or "daughter" to "child" "grandson" or "granddaughter" to "grandchild" "stepson/step-son" or "stepdaughter/step-daughter" to "stepchild" "son-in-law" or "daughter-in-law" to "child-in-law" "brother" or "sister" to "sibling" "stepbrother/step-brother" or "stepsister/step-sister" to "stepsibling" "brother-in-law" or "sister-in-law" to "sibling-in-law" "half-brother" or "half-sister" to "half-sibling" "widow" or "widower" to "surviving spouse"

  • 2.3.6. Use accurate auxiliary (helping) verb The meaning of each regulatory provision depends on the auxiliary or helper verb the drafter of the regulation uses. The helper verb (i.e., "will," "may," "shall," or "must") communicates the degree of requirement for the verb it is "helping."
  • 1. May. In the affirmative, "may" allows the action of a verb, but does not require that action; that is, "may" expresses a privilege not a mandate. When an individual is authorized but not ordered to act, "may" is appropriate. In the negative "may not" is the strongest expression of prohibition of the action of a verb. A permittee may allow access to facilities beyond hours of operation. A licensee may not transfer a license without approval from the board.
  • 2. Will. "Will" states the future tense (either affirmatively, or negatively when "not" is added) of the verb it is helping and carries no obligation for action upon that verb. For example, if 11VAC10-20 is repealed, the replacement chapter is numbered 11VAC10-21.

C. The agency creates only one RIS project to repeal and replace a chapter of VAC. The agency must include in the summary submitted with the regulatory text (i) the reasons for repealing the current chapter and replacing it with the new chapter and (ii) the major differences between the two chapters.

D. The repeal and replacement are considered one regulatory action. For example, it is not necessary to obtain separate letters from the Office of the Attorney General for the repeal and the replacement chapters; one letter may verify statutory authority for both actions.

  • 3.2.2. Repealing a section.

A. When repealing an individual section, RIS will strike through the catchline, the full text of the section, and the statutory authority, but not the section number or the historical notes, and the word "Repealed" is underscored, placed between parentheses, and inserted after the stricken catchline.

EXAMPLE 12VAC30-110-890. Resources transferred pursuant to § 1917 of the Act. (Repealed.) Provided transfers are made within one month of the initial determination of eligibility or within the protected period specified in 12VAC30-110-860, resources held by an institutionalized spouse shall not be counted in determining continuing eligibility when § 1917 transfers are made to parties for which there is no penalty for failure to receive equitable value, or transfer for which equitable value is received. Statutory Authority § 28.2-201 of the Code of Virginia. Historical Notes Derived from Virginia Register Volume 23, Issue 19, eff. May 1, 2007. B. If a section is repealed, the section number is not used again, and sections following a repealed section are not renumbered. Content of a section must stay basically the same. If the basic topic of a section is changed, rather than strike the catchline and text and replace it with new language, repeal the section and insert a new section following the repealed section or in the appropriate location. Once a section number has been used, it can neither be used again nor reserved to be used again. Chapters are set up with sections numbered in multiples of 10 so that the numbers in between may be used to amend the chapter at a later time.

  • 3.3. Adding a new section or chapter to the Virginia Administrative Code.
  • 3.3.1. Adding a chapter.

When an agency must add a new chapter to the agency's regulatory catalog, consult the Registrar's Office regarding the numbering of that chapter. Longevity of the agency's catalog of regulations must be considered. That is, future need to repeal and replace existing chapters or add more chapters that topically should be inserted within (instead of at the end of) the existing chapters must be taken into account.

All of the text of a new chapter is underlined.

  • 3.3.2. Adding a section.

To a more limited degree, adding a new section to a chapter also requires planning and consideration. The new section should relate topically to the sections surrounding it, leave sufficient room to allow the chapter to expand, and consider the logic of the new section's placement: Does it have general application or limited application? Does it expand privileges (or requirements) or contract them? All of the text of a new section is underlined.
